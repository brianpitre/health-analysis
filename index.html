Your advanced markers (ApoB, LDL-P, OxLDL, LP-PLA2) all confirm this isn't just a "fluffy LDL" situation—there is genuine, measurable atherogenic particle burden and vascular inflammation.

What's going well:

  • Triglycerides are excellent (50–54)—a strong metabolic health signal
  • HDL is good (79–109)
  • HDL efflux capacity is enhanced (17.1%)—your HDL is functionally effective
  • Insulin resistance score of 2 is outstanding
  • Homocysteine is normal

ApoB of 240 is the single most predictive lab value for cardiovascular events. It tells you how many atherogenic particles are in your blood. Yours is 2.7x the upper limit. Oxidized LDL >115 means those particles are undergoing oxidation—the process that initiates plaque formation. LP-PLA2 at 214 indicates active vascular inflammation. The trend is relentlessly upward with no sign of plateau.

The "lean mass hyper-responder" argument: You likely fit the LMHR profile (lean, active, low-carb, low triglycerides, high HDL). There is a hypothesis in the low-carb community that this lipid pattern is benign. However, LMHR study data so far has shown increased coronary artery calcium and subclinical atherosclerosis in this population. Your OxLDL and LP-PLA2 being elevated further weaken the "it's fine" position.

Recommendation: Get a coronary artery calcium (CAC) score and/or CT angiography (CCTA) to see if plaque is actually forming. This converts the debate from theoretical to concrete. If there is measurable plaque, dietary modification or pharmacological intervention becomes clearly indicated.

Your serum creatinine has been elevated (1.27–1.48) and eGFR as low as 59 (borderline Stage 3 CKD). This looks alarming on paper.

However, you take 10g of creatine monohydrate daily (2–3x the standard dose). Creatine is metabolized to creatinine. Combined with ~190 lbs of lean muscle mass, your serum creatinine is almost certainly elevated from supplementation + muscle mass, NOT kidney damage. This is confirmed by your cystatin C-based eGFR of 110, which is completely normal and unaffected by creatine intake.

The BUN spikes (38–45) correlate with 200g/day protein intake and possible dehydration.

Recommendation: Continue using cystatin C-based eGFR for monitoring. Stay well hydrated. Consider reducing creatine to 5g/day (still fully effective) to make standard labs more interpretable.

5. Sleep Duration

6.5 hours is below the recommended 7–9 hours for adults. For someone training 6 days/week at your intensity, sleep is arguably the most important recovery tool.

Insufficient sleep impairs testosterone production (critical during your HPG recovery), increases cortisol, reduces insulin sensitivity, impairs muscle recovery, and is independently associated with cardiovascular risk.

Recommendation: Target 7–8 hours. This is free, requires no supplements, and would likely improve every other metric you care about.
